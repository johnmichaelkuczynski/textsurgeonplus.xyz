11.1 A state of affairs \(e\) is evidence of a state of affairs \(e^*\) if and only if \(e\) and \(e^*\) are causally related in a way that gives knowledge of \(e\) reason to believe in \(e^*\).
12.0 Causal series, structure-preserving series, and information-bearing series are distinct.
12.1 A causal series consists of events \(e_1\) through \(e_n\) such that each event causes the next.
12.2 A structure-preserving series is a series in which the first event transmits its structure to the final event.
12.3 An information-bearing series is a structure-preserving series.
13.0 All information is evidence, but not all evidence is information.
13.1 Information is evidence because information-bearing series preserve structure.
13.2 Evidence need not be information because causal dependence need not preserve structure.
14.0 Information-transmission is the basis of knowledge.
14.1 Sense-perceptions are isomorphs of the objects they represent.
14.2 A perception-like experience is veridical if and only if it inherits its structure from its object.
14.3 Perception-based beliefs are accurate only to the extent that perceptions inherit the structures of their external counterparts.
14.4 A belief is knowledge only to the extent that it is linked to its corresponding reality by an information-transmissive series.
14.5 Statistical reliability does not constitute knowledge.
15.0 Causation constitutes temporal precedence.
15.1 An event \(e\) precedes an event \(e^*\) if and only if a possible causal process begins with \(e\) and ends with \(e^*\).
15.2 Two events are simultaneous if and only if no such causal process exists between them.
16.0 Continuity constitutes causation and information-transmission.
16.1 Effects are continuations of their causes.
16.2 An event transmits information about its cause to the extent that it inherits its structure from that cause.
16.3 Structure-preserving causation depends on the preservation of relations among concurrent causal lines.
16.4 Structure-preserving effects can reveal their causes without circumstance-specific causal knowledge.
16.5 Non-structure-preserving effects require ad hoc causal knowledge.
17.0 Knowledge begins with information-transmission and extends to evidential dependence.
17.1 Our thoughts inherit their structures from our perceptions.
17.2 Initial information-transmissive knowledge enables knowledge of non-information-transmissive evidential relations.
17.3 Knowledge of evidential relations therefore presupposes knowledge acquired through information-transmission.
